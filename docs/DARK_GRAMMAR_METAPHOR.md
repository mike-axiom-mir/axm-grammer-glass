# Dark Grammar — speculative analogy, not a cosmology claim

Grammar Glass uses **Dark Grammar** as a viewer term for recorded structure whose effects are visible in the field but which has not resolved into an explicit compatible formation or creation path.

The lens currently highlights recorded relationships such as:

- `UNRESOLVED_NEIGHBOURHOOD`
- `CONTRAST`
- `REPULSION`
- `BOUNDARY`
- contact-memory paths that pass through those regions

The analogy to cosmological dark matter is intentionally limited:

- In cosmology, dark matter is inferred from gravitational and large-scale-structure effects even though its microscopic nature is still unknown.
- In Grammar Glass, Dark Grammar is inferred only from **already recorded structural metadata and relationships** in the loaded snapshot.
- Grammar Glass does **not** claim that physical dark matter is software, code, grammar, information, unexpressed creation, or evidence for any computational-universe theory.

A useful speculative sentence for design discussion is:

> **Visible creation is structure that has crossed into an expressed form; Dark Grammar is structure whose influence is visible but whose creation connection remains unresolved.**

That sentence describes the Grammar Glass model only. It is not presented as physical truth.

The design reason for keeping the metaphor is that it gives the viewer a memorable way to inspect influence without prematurely converting uncertainty into a positive compatibility claim. The unknown stays visible instead of being designed away.

Truth boundary:

- analogy is not evidence
- unresolved does not mean hidden solution
- influence does not mean equivalence
- proximity does not mean causation
- Dark Grammar does not rank structures
- Dark Grammar does not create candidates automatically
- no claim about real dark matter is made
